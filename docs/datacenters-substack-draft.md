## 47 data centers, and where Austin's line is drawn

Every data center that is running, being built, approved, proposed, rumored
or cancelled in Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop and Caldwell counties, on
one map, with the power and water layers underneath. Snapshot of
September 12, 2026. The map is at anatomy.city/datacenters; every dot
carries its sources.

**The count**

| Status | Sites |
|---|---|
| Operational | 12 |
| Under construction | 12 |
| Approved or planned | 8 |
| Announced or proposed | 8 |
| Rumored | 4 |
| Cancelled or stalled | 3 |

**What the map shows**

- **The big projects are outside Austin Energy.** The purple line is the
  city utility's territory. Inside it, a data center buys power from the
  city, which sets the rates and approves the load. Every campus in
  Williamson County sits outside that line, in Oncor's part of the ERCOT
  market, where the customer signs its own supply contract. That boundary
  is one reason the largest projects cluster north and east.
- **Round Rock to Taylor is the corridor.** Skybox's PowerCampus in Hutto
  plans 600 MW on 220 acres. KDC's Project Comal next to Samsung in Taylor
  is 360 MW. Sabey and Switch are building in Round Rock. Samsung's fab
  brought 345 kV lines along US 79 and SH 130, land is cheap, and Hutto,
  Round Rock, Taylor and Georgetown all offer tax incentives.
- **The largest numbers are south and east.** CloudBurst's campus in San
  Marcos is listed at 1.2 GW, under construction. Tract's technology park
  in Caldwell County is listed at 4,000 MW on nearly 3,000 acres. Prime,
  Edged and EdgeConneX are all building in Caldwell and Bastrop counties.
- **Where the grid is slow, developers propose their own gas plants.**
  477 MW for Project Mustang near Taylor, 710 MW for Pacifico Energy in
  Bastrop County, 1.2 GW for CloudBurst in San Marcos.
- **Water is the harder limit.** The corridor sits in no groundwater
  conservation district, so nothing limits pumping there. Hutto's campuses
  run on city water; the Central Texas Data Center Tracker estimates 5 to
  8 million gallons a day for Skybox at full build. Newer projects promise
  closed-loop cooling.
- **Opposition works, sometimes.** San Marcos denied Highlander's 380 MW
  project in February after nine hours of testimony, then banned data
  centers in every zoning district in June. Sabey's San Marcos tract
  stalled with it. In Hutto, Zydeco withdrew a 70 MW project in April
  after 55 opposition letters at Planning and Zoning.

**Caveats.** Rural campuses are placed by road or tract description, not
by parcel; each popup says how much to trust the point. Statuses are as
reported on September 12 and change fast. Sources are company
announcements, council records and local reporting, listed per site.
